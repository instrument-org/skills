Should I…?

Should we move 40 people from Slack to Microsoft Teams?

Teams is already paid for inside your Microsoft 365 licenses, so the switch reads like free money. It is $4,200 a year against a one-time cost of roughly twice that, plus a set of vendor channels that do not survive the move. The number says wait. The checklist says when to stop waiting.

Short answer

Not yet

Revisit at the Slack renewal, with the checklist below filled in. Three ticks and it flips.

What you told us

Forty people, all on Microsoft 365, all on Slack today, and a bill for one chat tool that the other license already covers.

What this assumes

Slack Pro at $8.75 a seat a month billed yearly, so $4,200. A Business Standard tenant bought in the United States, where Teams carries no separate license. Eleven Slack apps installed and three Slack Connect channels with outside companies.

What it depends on

Tick what is true today. Three or more and the answer moves to yes.

By what kind of company you are

If you are mostly engineers

No

Stay. Deploy bots, alert routing, and the GitHub app are where Slack earns its seat, and rebuilding them in Teams is a month of someone's time nobody budgeted.

If you are mostly sales, ops, and finance

Yes

Your day is already in Outlook and Excel. Chat beside the calendar is a small step, and Slack was the tab people forgot to open. Go at renewal, with a 30-day dual run.

If half the company already takes calls in Teams

Yes, with a date

You are paying for two chat tools and using both badly. Pick the Slack shutoff date first, then start; without the date this becomes the six-month dual run below.

If finance is driving it for the $4,200

Not yet

Year one is a wash after migration time, and year two's $4,200 buys less than the vendor channels cost to lose. Ask finance for the three-year number, with the 80 hours priced in.

Questions people ask

Can we run both for a while?
Thirty days with a shutoff date, yes. Open-ended, no; that is how a company ends up paying for two.
Does Teams really cost nothing extra?
On a Business Standard tenant bought in the United States, yes. Subscriptions bought since April 2024 in some regions carry Teams as a separate $5.25 a seat; check the licenses page before trusting the zero.
Do we lose the history?
Slack Pro exports all of it, and a migration tool imports it as flat messages under each channel. Keep the export file either way.
Could we drop to Slack's free plan instead?
It hides messages older than 90 days and limits Connect, so it fails the same vendor-channel test Teams does, and saves the same $4,200.

What goes wrong

  1. 1Connect channels do not move. Each becomes email, a guest account, or a shared channel the other company's admin has to approve, which takes weeks when it happens at all.
  2. 2History flattens. Threads, reactions, and pins do not come across; files re-link. Budget about $1,500 for the tool and a week of complaints.
  3. 3The dual run with no end. Two chat tools for six months is the common failure: nobody knows where to post, and both go quiet.
  4. 4Tenant defaults. External and guest access are off in a fresh Teams admin center. A locked-down tenant blocks the vendor channels for weeks.
  5. 5The quiet quarter. Cross-team messages drop after a switch and take a quarter or two to recover. Do not move during a launch.

Revisit when

  • The renewal date is inside 90 days. Write it here.
  • Two of the three Connect channels have ended or moved.
  • The deploy bot and alert routing have a Teams version someone ran for a week.
  • A named owner has two clear weeks.

Next step

Spend an hour on the numbers

Download Slack's analytics page (active members by week, messages by channel, apps installed) and list each Connect channel with the company on the other side. It tells you which boxes you can tick today, and it is the same list a migration tool asks for later.
