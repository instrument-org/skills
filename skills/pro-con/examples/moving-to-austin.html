Pros and cons

Moving from Chicago to Austin, for a remote engineer with a partner and a dog

Both of you work from home, you rent, and the dog is sixty pounds and walks twice a day. The case for is money and winter; the case against is summer and a car. On the weights below it leans Austin, by less than the tax savings suggest.

What is being weighed

Now
A two-bedroom in Logan Square at about $2,400 a month, no car, the Blue Line two blocks away.
Considering
A two-bedroom with a yard in East Austin or Mueller at about $1,900 a month, seen this month.
Assumed
A $180,000 salary, both jobs fully remote, and family within a short flight of Chicago. Change these and the weights move.
Weight, set for your situation decides it matters minor

The case for Austin

  • No state income tax

    Illinois takes a flat 4.95 percent. On $180,000 that is about $8,900 a year, and it is the only line on this page that is pure arithmetic.

  • Rent is lower, for now

    About $500 a month less for a comparable two-bedroom, roughly $6,000 a year. Austin rents fell from their 2022 peak as new buildings opened; the discount narrows as they fill.

  • Winter mostly goes away

    January lows average about 18 degrees in Chicago and about 41 in Austin. The 6 a.m. dog walk stops being the worst part of the day for four months of the year.

  • A dog city with yards at your rent

    Off-leash at Red Bud Isle and Auditorium Shores, dogs on most patios, the Barton Creek trails inside the city, and a fenced yard is normal at $1,900 rather than a $600 upgrade.

  • A large remote-tech crowd

    Coworking within a bike ride, weekly meetups, and a lot of people in your exact situation. Worth something only if you will use it; remote work is remote in both cities.

The case against

  • Summer, with a dog

    Austin logged about 80 days at or above 100 degrees in 2023. From July through September the walks happen before 7 a.m. and after 8 p.m., and pavement burns paws by mid-morning. Chicago's worst week is Austin's average July.

  • You will need a car

    Cap Metro does not replace the Blue Line; most errands are a drive. One mid-priced used car, insured and parked, runs about $550 a month, which takes back about $6,600 of the tax saving. A second car takes back the rest.

  • Leaving a life on foot

    Groceries, friends, and the L within a ten-minute walk. Austin has a few neighborhoods like that, and they are small and priced like it. Hard to put a number on, so the weight is yours to move.

  • A smaller airport

    O'Hare flies nonstop to well over 200 cities; Austin to around 90. Family visits become a $350 fare with one connection instead of a Friday-afternoon nonstop.

  • Property tax, if you ever buy

    Texas has no income tax and about a 1.8 percent property tax. On a $550,000 house that is around $10,000 a year, which hands the income-tax saving straight back. As renters it is already in the rent.

  • Cedar fever

    Mountain cedar pollen from December to February hits many newcomers within a few years, and it is a real cold, not a sniffle. Antihistamines handle most cases.

Good for

A couple whose life happens at home and outdoors: two remote jobs, a dog that wants a yard and a creek, no objection to driving, and a plan to bank the difference rather than spend it. If you were already pricing cars and eyeing a house with a fence, Austin gives you both at a discount.

Bad for

Anyone whose Chicago is on foot: the L, the bar three doors down, a friend group within a mile. Austin sells that in two or three neighborhoods at Chicago prices. Also anyone who wilts above 95 degrees, or whose family trips have to stay a nonstop flight away.

Bottom line

Move if the yard, the winter, and about $8,000 a year after one car are what you are after. Stay if what you would miss is the walk to everything, because Austin does not sell that at any rent.

Before you sign anything: spend a week there in August with the dog, price the car, and check whether either employer has a rule about the state you work from.
