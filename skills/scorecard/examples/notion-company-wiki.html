Scorecard

Notion as the company wiki for a 40-person team

Graded on six dimensions against what a 40-person company needs from the place it keeps its policies, runbooks, and decisions. It earns a B+: adopt it this quarter, with one owner for structure and one rule about where the current version of anything lives.

B+

Overall

Good enough to adopt now: strong on writing and habit, weak on finding things.

Plain mean of six grades on the four-point scale, 3.27, rounded to the nearest letter. Nothing below a C+, one A. The search grade is the one the team will feel daily.

By dimension, most important first

  1. Editing and structure

    A

    Pages, databases, and synced blocks cover every page type a wiki needs. Templates for meeting notes, decisions, and runbooks took one afternoon to set up in the trial workspace, and nobody asked how to use them.

  2. Adoption and habit

    A-

    Fourteen of the forty already keep personal notes in Notion. The trial workspace had 31 weekly editors by week three with no mandate; the current wiki, a Drive folder, had four in the same weeks.

  3. Search and findability

    C+

    Search weights titles and recency over body text. In the 900-page trial import, "expense policy" returned the 2022 draft above the current one, and "on-call" found the runbook only after it was retitled to contain the phrase.

  4. Permissions and admin

    B

    Teamspaces map cleanly onto the five departments. There is no read-only default for a teamspace, so any member can edit any policy page unless the page is locked by hand, and locking is per page.

  5. Integrations

    B+

    Slack unfurls, Google Drive embeds, and GitHub links work out of the box. Jira needs a third-party sync, and the API is an engineer's tool rather than an admin's.

  6. Cost at 40 seats

    B+

    Business plan at about $18 per seat per month billed annually, roughly $8,600 a year. Plus, at $10, lacks SAML and 90-day page history, and both matter for a wiki that holds policy.

Strengths

  • Writing in it is pleasant enough that people do it without being asked. The trial's edit count is the strongest evidence on this page.
  • Databases turn index pages into views that maintain themselves: a policy table filtered by owner and review date replaces the "who owns this" spreadsheet.
  • Synced blocks keep the ten most-copied fragments (office address, holidays, the escalation ladder) in one place.
  • Fourteen people already know it, so onboarding is a link rather than a session.

Weaknesses

  • Search misfires on any term that appears in more than a few pages. Plan on a curated index page per department and a naming rule for drafts.
  • No read-only default. Someone owns locking and archiving, or the policy pages drift within a quarter.
  • Page history has no diff view; auditing what changed in the leave policy means reading two versions side by side.
  • Export is Markdown and CSV with relations flattened. Leaving later is a project, not a click.
