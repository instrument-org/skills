Overall

By dimension

Mechanical condition x2

Inspection at an independent Subaru shop found no CVT shudder on a cold start, no oil consumption between changes, and dry head gaskets. Front pads at 4 mm: budget $350 within six months.

Price against the market

$19,500 sits about $700 above the private-party median for a one-owner 2019 2.5i Premium near 60,000 miles within 150 miles. Dealer listings run $20,500 to $21,900, so the ask is dealer-adjacent.

History and ownership

One owner, clean title, no accident or damage records, and 11 service entries on the history report including the 30,000 and 60,000-mile services. Registered in a dry-climate state the whole time.

Known-issue exposure

The 2019 model year carries a settlement on windshield cracking and a campaign on battery drain. This windshield is original with no chips; the battery was replaced in 2023 per the receipts. The powertrain warranty ended at 60,000 miles.

Running costs

EPA 28 mpg combined on regular fuel. CVT fluid is due about now and is not on the receipts: about $250. Parts and labor pricing is average for the class; tires are a matched set with 6/32 left.

Fit for your use

All-wheel drive and 8.7 inches of clearance for the cabin road, 73 cubic feet with the seats down, and roof rails that take the ski box. The 2.5 is adequate rather than quick with four people and gear aboard.

Walk away if

  • The seller will not allow a second inspection at a shop you choose, or the car you see differs from the VIN on the report.
  • The transmission shudders or flares between 20 and 40 mph on your own cold-start drive. Out of warranty, a CVT on this car is a $4,000 to $7,000 repair.
  • The oil is more than a quart low against the sticker's last change date, or the coolant overflow shows any oil film.
  • The price will not move below $19,200. A dealer car at that money comes with a warranty; this one does not.

Strengths

  • Paper trail from one owner with both major services done on time.
  • Clean inspection on the three things that kill this platform: CVT, oil use, head gaskets.
  • The known-issue battery has already been replaced, and the windshield is intact.

Weaknesses

  • Priced like a dealer car without a dealer's warranty or recourse.
  • Just past the powertrain warranty, with the CVT fluid service still owed.
  • Original windshield on a model year with a cracking settlement: a $600 to $900 bill if it goes.
