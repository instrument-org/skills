Where to look for a flat, for a job at King’s Cross

The Victoria and Northern lines both run into King’s Cross, so the short answer is to live on one of them. Minutes are station to station at half past eight on a weekday.

Under ten minutes, no change Direct, under twenty minutes One change of train The office

King’s Cross
  1. 1

    Highbury

    3 min

    From Highbury & Islington · Victoria line, no change

    Georgian and Victorian terraces around Highbury Fields, Upper Street’s restaurants a walk away, and the Arsenal ground at the top of the hill. One stop from the office.

    Google Maps

  2. 2

    Stroud Green and Finsbury Park

    5 min

    From Finsbury Park · Victoria line, no change

    Stroud Green Road’s long run of shops and cafés, with the park at the foot and Crouch End up the hill. The station also carries the Piccadilly line and trains north.

    Google Maps

  3. 3

    Kentish Town and Tufnell Park

    9 min

    From Kentish Town · Northern line, no change

    Quieter Victorian streets between Camden’s noise and Hampstead Heath, which is a walk. Thameslink runs from the same station, one stop to St Pancras.

    Google Maps

  4. 4

    Dalston and De Beauvoir

    14 min

    From Dalston Kingsland · Overground to Highbury, then the Victoria line

    The liveliest evenings of the six, Ridley Road market, and the one place here that means a change of train. De Beauvoir’s squares are the quiet end.

    Google Maps

  5. 5

    Walthamstow

    16 min

    From Walthamstow Central · Victoria line, no change

    The top of the Victoria line: the Village’s old streets, the marshes and the wetlands, and a train every couple of minutes that runs straight to the office.

    Google Maps

  6. 6

    Brixton

    17 min

    From Brixton · Victoria line, no change

    The other end of the same line, south of the river: the market and the arches, Brockwell Park, and a direct train, though the platform is crowded by half past eight.

    Google Maps
