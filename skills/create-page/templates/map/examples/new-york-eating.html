Where to eat on a first weekend in New York

A morning, a midday and an evening each covered, most of it a walk between Union Square and Canal Street, and two worth the train to Brooklyn. Tap an entry to find its pin.

  1. Morning
  2. 1

    Russ & Daughters Cafe

    Lower East Side · appetizing, at a table

    The 1914 shop is on Houston Street; this is the café round the corner where you can sit down to the same fish. A bagel with nova and cream cheese, or the smoked fish board if there are two of you.

    russanddaughters.comGoogle Maps

  3. 2

    Ess-a-Bagel

    Midtown East · bagel shop

    Hand-rolled, boiled and baked on the premises, and larger than a bagel has any right to be. Take a number, order at the counter, eat it on the walk to Grand Central.

    ess-a-bagel.comGoogle Maps

  4. 3

    Levain Bakery

    Upper West Side · bakery

    The six-ounce chocolate chip walnut cookie, warm, is the thing that made the name. Ten minutes from the Museum of Natural History, so it pairs with a morning there.

    levainbakery.comGoogle Maps

  5. Midday
  6. 4

    Katz’s Delicatessen

    Lower East Side · deli since 1888

    Pastrami on rye, hand-cut at the counter while you watch. Keep the ticket they hand you at the door; you pay on it on the way out, and losing it is a fine.

    katzsdelicatessen.comGoogle Maps

  7. 5

    Joe’s Pizza

    Greenwich Village · slice shop

    A plain slice, folded, eaten standing at the counter or on the sidewalk on Carmine Street. The original of the branches, since 1975.

    joespizzanyc.comGoogle Maps

  8. 6

    Xi’an Famous Foods

    Chinatown, Bayard Street · noodles

    Spicy cumin lamb on hand-ripped noodles, wide and chewy, from a counter with a few stools. There are branches all over the city; this one is a short walk from Nom Wah.

    xianfoods.comGoogle Maps

  9. 7

    Nom Wah Tea Parlor

    Chinatown, Doyers Street · dim sum since 1920

    The oldest dim sum room in the city, on the crooked street. The original egg roll and the shrimp rice rolls; order from the sheet, no carts.

    nomwah.comGoogle Maps

  10. Evening
  11. 8

    Grand Central Oyster Bar

    Midtown, inside the terminal · seafood since 1913

    Sit at the counter under the tiled vaults and order the oyster pan roast, which is made in front of you. Dinner in a train station that is worth going to for its own sake.

    oysterbarny.comGoogle Maps

  12. 9

    Balthazar

    SoHo · brasserie

    The room is the point: mirrors, red banquettes, the noise of a Paris brasserie transplanted to Spring Street. Steak frites, and book a table.

    balthazarny.comGoogle Maps

  13. 10

    Veselka

    East Village · Ukrainian diner, open late

    Pierogi and borscht at any hour, in the diner that has fed the East Village since 1954. The late stop after everything else has closed.

    veselka.comGoogle Maps

  14. Worth the train
  15. 11

    Peter Luger Steak House

    Williamsburg, Brooklyn · steakhouse since 1887

    The porterhouse for two, sliced and sizzling on the plate, and the thick bacon to start. Under the Williamsburg Bridge; it takes cash or its own card, so check before you go.

    peterluger.comGoogle Maps

  16. 12

    Di Fara Pizza

    Midwood, Brooklyn · pizzeria since 1965

    A square slice, or whatever is coming out, from the shop the De Marco family has run on Avenue J for sixty years. Expect a line, and the longest ride on this page.

    difarapizzany.comGoogle Maps
