TLDR brief · The EU AI Act for a 30-person B2B SaaS

You are a deployer, not a high‑risk provider. Two obligations land by August 2026, and both are about a week of work.

For the founder or ops lead of a 30-person B2B SaaS whose product drafts, summarizes, and searches with a hosted LLM and sells into the EU. Two minutes to read; the long notes are linked at the end.

  • The big picture: The Act sorts AI by risk, and nearly all of its text is written for the top tiers. A drafting assistant, a support summarizer, or a semantic search box is limited risk at most, which leaves you with disclosure and training duties, not audits.
  • Your role: Calling a hosted model makes the vendor the provider of a general-purpose AI model and you the provider of the AI system built on it. Why it matters: the model's documentation, copyright, and safety duties are the vendor's; the duties on the feature you ship are yours.
  • Already in force: AI literacy (Article 4) has applied since February 2, 2025. Staff who build, sell, or operate AI features need training proportionate to their role. A recorded two-hour session covers it; no session at all is the gap a regulator would find first.
  • The date that matters: August 2, 2026, when the transparency rules (Article 50) apply. People must be told when they are interacting with an AI, and generated text published to inform the public must be marked as generated. It is the only obligation that touches your product's UI.
  • The catch: If any feature ranks, filters, or scores people in hiring, credit, education, insurance, or essential services, it is high risk under Annex III, and the duties jump to a risk management system, logging, human oversight, a conformity assessment, and registration. Check every roadmap feature against Annex III before building it, not after.
  • By the numbers: Fines run up to 15 million euros or 3 percent of global turnover for most breaches, and up to 35 million or 7 percent for prohibited practices. Small and medium enterprises pay whichever of the two figures is lower. Proportionate, and still not small.
  • What's next: Harmonized standards for high-risk systems are still in draft, and a Commission proposal to push the high-risk dates by about a year is in the legislative process. Watch it; do not plan on it.

What applies when

Date What starts For you
Feb 2, 2025 Prohibited practices, AI literacy Train the team and keep the attendance list.
Aug 2, 2025 General-purpose model duties, governance, penalties Nothing direct. Ask your model vendor for its documentation and file it.
Aug 2, 2026 Transparency (Article 50), Annex III high risk, most of the rest Disclose the AI in the UI and mark generated content. The high-risk track only if a feature is Annex III.
Aug 2, 2027 High-risk AI inside regulated products (Annex I) Not you, unless you sell into medical devices or machinery.

Do

  • Keep a one-page AI inventory: feature, model, vendor, purpose, risk tier.
  • Add an "AI-generated" label to chat replies and generated output before August 2026.
  • Run one AI literacy session this quarter and record who attended.
  • Check each roadmap feature against Annex III at planning time.

Do not

  • Hire a conformity consultant before you have a high-risk feature.
  • Treat it as GDPR with a new name; the triggers and the regulator differ.
  • Ship a feature that ranks candidates or scores credit without a legal review.
  • Assume a mostly US customer base exempts you; the Act applies where the output is used.

Go deeper
