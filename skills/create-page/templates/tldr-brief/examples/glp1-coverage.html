TLDR brief Benefits planning · For the HR lead · Two minutes

Cover GLP-1s for weight loss next year, inside a managed program. The market is moving to guardrails, not to yes or no.

Employers are adding coverage with conditions attached, net prices are falling, and employees are asking. The cost is real; the cost of adding it and then pulling it is worse.

The big picture.Coverage is rising, not retreating. About 44 percent of employers with 500 or more workers cover GLP-1s for obesity this year, up from 34 percent a year earlier, and nearly every new adopter attached conditions. The question for next year is which conditions, not whether.

By the numbers.Net cost after rebates runs about $600 to $800 per user per month. On a 500-member plan with open coverage, four to six percent of members start within the year, which is roughly $170,000 to $250,000. Why it matters: it is now the largest single line in pharmacy trend, ahead of specialty drugs at many plans.

The shift.Plans have moved from yes or no to yes, with: a BMI floor of 30, or 27 with a related condition, prior authorization, a required lifestyle program, and a review at six months. Plans with all four report uptake at about half the open-coverage rate and similar weight outcomes for the people who stay on.

The catch.Persistence is poor. About half of people who start stop within a year, and weight returns when they do. Paying for the drug without the program buys the spend and not the outcome, and the program is the cheap half.

Yes, but.Pulling coverage costs more than never adding it. Plans that dropped obesity coverage after a year report it as the top complaint at open enrollment and a named reason in exit interviews. Design next year's terms as ones you can hold for three.

What's next.Prices are moving down. Cash-pay vials at about $500 a month, Medicare's negotiated price arriving in 2027, and oral GLP-1s expected in 2026 should cut net prices 20 to 40 percent within two years. Why it matters: the terms you set now can loosen later; terms set loose now cannot tighten without a fight.

The alternative.A carve-out vendor that bundles the drug with coaching at a capped per-member fee is the fastest way to a managed program. Check that its clinicians prescribe on label and that its data flows back to your carrier, or you will pay twice for the same member.

A 500-member plan, under three designs

Design Uptake Annual net cost Note
Open, BMI criteria only 4 to 6% $170k to $250k Fastest uptake, half gone by month twelve.
Managed: prior auth, program, six-month review 2 to 3% $85k to $125k Same outcomes for those who stay; the default at large plans now.
Carve-out vendor, capped fee 1.5 to 2.5% $50k to $85k Quick to launch; check the prescribing and the data flow.

Estimates at $700 net per user per month; uptake ranges are what comparable plans reported.

Do

  • Put the BMI criteria in the plan document, not in a carrier memo.
  • Tie the six-month re-authorization to a documented result.
  • Pay for the lifestyle program, and require it.
  • Budget the high end of the managed range.

Do not

  • Cover the drug without the program.
  • Add terms you may have to pull in a year.
  • Let a vendor prescribe off label to hit its price.
  • Count compounded versions as a budget option; they are leaving the market.
