Usually goes wrong

  1. 1Success metric. Named after launch, it measures nothing. Write the number down before the flag flips and check that the dashboard shows it at zero today.
  2. 2Kill switch. A flag that has never been flipped off has never been tested. Flip it, watch the UI, flip it back.
  3. 3Load test. Peak is not average. Take the busiest hour of the busiest day and double it; that is the number the test has to hold.
  4. 4Migration. Run it on a copy of production, time it, then run the down migration too. The one nobody rehearsed is the one that fails at hour one.
  5. 5SSO and SCIM. Enterprise accounts sit behind SSO and provisioned roles. New permissions default to the wrong value there more often than anywhere else.
  6. 6Help article. Published early and visible, it leaks the launch; published late, support has nothing to link. Hidden-until-launch is the setting to check.
  7. 7Launch post. The post goes out on time and the flag flips late. Put the flag flip on the same calendar invite as the post, with the same owner.
  8. 8Screenshots. Taken from staging last week, they show a button that was renamed since. Retake them the day before, from production.
  9. 9In-app announcement. Shown to plans without access, it writes the first tickets. Check the targeting against the plan gate, not against the flag.
  10. 10Privacy review. Booked last, it is the item that moves the date. Book it the week the design is final, not the week before launch.
Based on. A generic B2B launch with a feature flag, SSO customers, and a legal review step; owners are placeholders for a five-person launch team. Items that depend on your contracts or regions say so rather than assuming one setup. Prints on two sheets at Letter with the tips on the second. Illustrative: items are representative, not verified.
