A laptop for 4K editing in DaVinci Resolve under $1,800

Four laptops a film student could buy this semester, scored on six weighted criteria. The MacBook Pro 14 with the M4 wins at 79.0 of 100, five points clear of the Asus ProArt P16, and it holds the lead when price is weighted double. Only a student who edits at a desk and never unplugs should buy the Lenovo Legion instead.

The goal

One laptop for a second-year film student who cuts 4K H.264 and BRAW timelines in DaVinci Resolve Studio, grades on the same screen, and carries it between classes, the library, and a shared edit suite. It has to last three years of coursework and a thesis film.

  • Under $1,800 before tax, with student pricing allowed.
  • A 4K timeline plays without proxies, and a ten-minute export finishes in a lunch break.
  • A screen that can be trusted for a first grading pass.
  • A class day on battery, with Resolve open for part of it.

Criteria and weights

Weights sum to 100 and were set from the goal before any laptop was scored. The bar is each weight against the heaviest.

Resolve performance 30
Timeline playback and export speed are the whole reason to buy a new machine. Heaviest by a margin, but not a majority, because a fast laptop with a poor screen fails the grading half of the course.
Display for grading 25
P3 coverage, brightness, and a factory calibration the student can rely on until they can afford a reference monitor.
Battery and portability 15
Carried daily and used unplugged in class. Half the weight of performance because the heavy exports happen at a desk.
Price against the cap 15
Everything on the list is under $1,800, so price separates a comfortable buy from one that eats the software budget. Doubled in the second sensitivity pass.
Ports and storage 10
An SD slot and a fast 1 TB SSD save a dongle and an external drive on every shoot day.
Repairability and warranty 5
Three years is long enough for a battery or a fan to go. Lightest weight because campus repair and a warranty cover most of it.

What changes if the weights move

If the laptop never leaves the desk

Resolve 40 · Display 25 · Battery 0 · Price 20 · Ports 10 · Repair 5

Drop battery to zero, give its points to performance and price, and the Legion wins: Legion 76.5, ProArt 75.5, MacBook 74.5, Swift 69.5. It is the only weighting in this pass that moves the MacBook off the top, and it describes a student with a fixed edit bay, not one who carries the machine to class.

If price counts double

Resolve 30 · Display 10 · Battery 15 · Price 30 · Ports 10 · Repair 5

Move 15 points from display to price and the MacBook still leads: MacBook 73.0, Legion 71.0, ProArt 68.0, Swift 68.0. The lead narrows from five points to two, so a student who also has to buy a Resolve Studio license this year should read the Legion's row carefully before deciding.

The ProArt loses to the MacBook on every criterion except repairability, so no weighting of these six makes it win; it is the runner-up because it is the best Windows machine here, not because a weight is close. The Swift X never places above third.
