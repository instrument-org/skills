Recommendation with criteria

A second office for a sixty-person software company

Denver, Raleigh, Austin, and Salt Lake City, scored on six criteria under a weighting the leadership team agreed on before any city was scored. Raleigh wins at 80.0 of 100, eight points ahead of Austin, and keeps the lead under every weighting tried except one: when talent and business climate together carry 65 of the points, Austin edges ahead by a point and a half.

Winner

Raleigh, North Carolina

80.0 / 100

Won on cost, travel, and time zone; second on talent; lost only livability to Salt Lake City.

The goal

A second office that can hold forty people within three years, most of them engineers, in a city that hires well against the big tech offices already there. Headquarters stays in New York, the leadership team visits monthly, and the lease budget is $1.2 million a year all in. The office opens next fall, so incentives that take two years to negotiate do not count.

Why these weights

  • Engineering talent, 30. The office exists to hire twenty-five engineers. Nothing else matters if that fails.
  • Cost, 25. Salaries plus rent, as a share of the $1.2 million. Second because the whole plan rests on it costing less than growing in New York.
  • Travel to HQ, 15. Monthly visits by four people, and customers who fly in. Nonstop count and flight time from New York.
  • Livability, 15. Housing cost and the outdoors, because both decide whether a New York hire will relocate.
  • Business climate, 10. State taxes and the paperwork of a second entity. Real money, but a one-time cost.
  • Time zone, 5. Overlap with a New York standup. Lightest because the team already works across three zones with contractors.

Raleigh leads at 80.0; Austin is second at 72.0.

  • Denver. A good pool that three large employers already fish in: 7. Senior salaries near $165,000 and rent at $38 a square foot: 6. Many nonstops but four hours each: 7. The outdoors sells itself: 8.
  • Raleigh. Three research universities inside twenty miles and less competition per engineer than Austin: 8. Salaries near $150,000 and rent at $34: 8. Ninety minutes nonstop, a dozen a day: 9. Same time zone: 10.
  • Austin. The deepest pool by far, and the most bidders for it: 9. Salaries near $170,000 and rent at $48, the highest here: 5. No state income tax: 9. The summers and the housing run-up cost it on livability: 6.
  • Salt Lake City. A smaller pool, half of it already inside a few companies: 6. Salaries near $145,000 and rent at $32: 8. Five hours and a thin nonstop schedule: 5. Cheapest housing here and mountains at the end of the street: 9.

What changes if the weights move

Growth mode

Talent 45 · Cost 10 · Travel 10 · Livability 10 · Business 20 · Time zone 5
The weighting for a company that has just raised and wants the deepest pool and the friendliest tax code. Austin takes it: Austin 80.5, Raleigh 79.0, Salt Lake City 68.0, Denver 67.5. The margin is a point and a half, which is inside the honest error of a 1 to 10 score, so under this weighting the two are a coin flip.

Lean year

Talent 20 · Cost 40 · Travel 10 · Livability 15 · Business 10 · Time zone 5
Cost above everything, as it would be if the next round slips. Raleigh holds: Raleigh 79.5, Salt Lake City 73.5, Austin 67.0, Denver 66.0. Salt Lake City moves to second and Austin drops to third, so a cost-first board would still land on Raleigh.

Never wins

Denver scores below Raleigh on every criterion except livability, where it trails Salt Lake City, so no weighting of these six puts it first. Salt Lake City wins only if livability carries more than half the points, which is not a weighting the leadership team would defend.

Sources.
