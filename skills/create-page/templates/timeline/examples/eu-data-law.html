Timeline

EU data law from 1995 to now, for a founder selling into Europe

Thirty-one years in three eras, and the handful of dates that changed what a company outside the EU has to do to sell to people inside it. Every entry ends with what it meant for a founder. The three turning points are the ones to read if you read nothing else.

Span
October 1995 to September 2026, plus two dates already on the calendar after that.
Whose story
A software company with no EU office and customers in the EU. The dates are the same for an EU company; the consequences read differently.
Why now
The AI Act's high-risk rules and the Digital Omnibus are moving the ground again in 2026, and both land on a founder's roadmap.

  1. 1995 to 2016 The directive years One rulebook per country, and a checkbox for the Atlantic.

  2. Oct 24, 1995

    Data Protection Directive 95/46/EC is adopted

    A directive, so each member state wrote its own version by 1998. Selling in five countries meant five registration regimes and five readings of what consent was.

  3. Jul 26, 2000

    Safe Harbor: the Commission's first adequacy decision for the US

    Self-certify with the US Commerce Department and EU data could sit on your US servers. For fifteen years this was a checkbox on a form.

  4. Jul 12, 2002

    ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC

    Marketing email to individuals needs opt-in consent unless they are already customers. The rule that makes cold outreach to a personal address risky in Europe dates from here, not from the GDPR.

  5. Nov 25, 2009

    Directive 2009/136/EC adds the cookie consent rule

    Storing anything on a visitor's device that is not strictly necessary needs informed consent, transposed by May 2011. The cookie banner is born, and it is still governed by this text.

  6. Jan 25, 2012

    The Commission proposes the General Data Protection Regulation

    Four years of negotiation followed. The two clauses that matter most to a founder outside the EU, the reach beyond its borders and the fine scaled to worldwide turnover, survived them.

  7. May 13, 2014

    Court of Justice, Google Spain: the right to be delisted

    A person can ask a search engine to drop results about them. The first sign the court would read the directive generously toward individuals and against the companies holding their data.

  8. Oct 6, 2015

    Court of Justice, Schrems I: Safe Harbor is invalid

    The checkbox disappeared overnight. Companies fell back on standard contractual clauses while Brussels and Washington negotiated a replacement.

  9. Jul 12, 2016

    Privacy Shield is adopted

    The replacement checkbox. It lasted four years, and the companies that certified under it learned to keep the clauses in a drawer anyway.

  10. 2016 to 2023 The GDPR years One law for the whole market, fines to make it stick, and the Atlantic reopened as a problem.

  11. Apr 27, 2016

    Regulation (EU) 2016/679, the GDPR, is adopted

    A regulation applies directly in every member state, no transposition. The two-year runway to May 2018 is when most companies first hired a privacy lawyer.

  12. Jan 10, 2017

    The Commission proposes an ePrivacy Regulation

    Meant to replace the 2002 directive and the cookie rule on the same day the GDPR applied. It stalled in Council for eight years and was withdrawn in 2025, so the cookie rule you follow today is still the 2009 text.

  13. May 25, 2018

    Turning point

    The GDPR applies

    One law across the EU and the EEA, and it reaches you wherever you are if you offer goods or services to people in the EU. A lawful basis for every processing purpose, a record of processing, requests answered within a month, breaches reported within 72 hours, a representative in the EU if you have no establishment there, and fines up to 4 percent of worldwide turnover. Every privacy program a founder builds today is built on this date.

  14. Jan 21, 2019

    CNIL fines Google 50 million euros

    The first large fine, for consent and transparency in account setup. Regulators showed they would reach for the top of the scale, and consent flows across the industry were redesigned that spring.

  15. Oct 1, 2019

    Court of Justice, Planet49: a pre-ticked box is not consent

    Cookie banners need a real choice. The banners with a reject button as prominent as the accept button date from this ruling and the regulator guidance that followed it.

  16. Jul 16, 2020

    Turning point

    Court of Justice, Schrems II: Privacy Shield is invalid

    Transfers to the US became your problem rather than a treaty's. Standard contractual clauses survived, but only with a transfer impact assessment and supplementary measures behind them. Every US subprocessor in your stack now needed paperwork, EU customers started asking where data lives, and "EU hosting" became a line on procurement questionnaires.

  17. Jun 4, 2021

    New standard contractual clauses, Decision (EU) 2021/914

    Modular clauses for four transfer scenarios, including processor to processor. The old clauses had to be replaced in every contract by December 27, 2022, which is why your vendor agreements all changed that year.

  18. May 22, 2023

    Irish DPC fines Meta 1.2 billion euros over US transfers

    The largest GDPR fine, for exactly the transfers Schrems II unsettled. Clauses without measures behind them were shown not to be enough, even for a company with every lawyer it could want.

  19. Jul 10, 2023

    EU-US Data Privacy Framework adequacy decision

    Transfers to certified US companies are lawful again without an assessment. The General Court upheld it in September 2025 and it is still under challenge, so the clauses stay in the drawer for the third time.

  20. 2023 to now The AI Act years The law turns from what your product holds to what it does, and then starts to simplify.

  21. Dec 13, 2023

    Data Act, Regulation (EU) 2023/2854, is adopted

    If your product is a connected device or a cloud service, users get a right to the data it generates and a right to switch providers. It applies from September 2025 and phases out switching charges after that.

  22. Feb 17, 2024

    Digital Services Act applies to every platform

    If users publish content on your service, you need a notice-and-action process and a named contact point. Most business software falls outside it; marketplaces, communities, and anything with a public feed do not.

  23. Aug 1, 2024

    Turning point

    The AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, enters into force

    The first law about what your product does rather than what data it holds. Prohibited practices apply from February 2, 2025, obligations for general-purpose models from August 2, 2025, and the high-risk rules were scheduled for August 2, 2026. If your product screens job applicants, scores creditworthiness, or decides who gets access to a service, it is on the high-risk list, and the work is conformity assessment, documentation, and human oversight, not a privacy policy.

  24. Feb 11, 2025

    The Commission withdraws the ePrivacy Regulation proposal

    Eight years without agreement. The cookie rule stays in the 2002 directive as amended in 2009, and the omnibus below is the next attempt to fix it.

  25. Sep 12, 2025

    The Data Act applies

    Contract terms on data access and on leaving your service had to be in place from this date. For a cloud product this mostly means an export path that works and terms that say so.

  26. Nov 19, 2025

    The Commission proposes the Digital Omnibus

    Would narrow what counts as personal data, move cookie consent into the GDPR with a browser-signal route, and push the AI Act's high-risk dates back. It needs Parliament and Council to pass, so every part of it is a proposal until they do.

  27. Aug 2, 2026

    AI Act high-risk obligations, as originally scheduled verify

    The omnibus proposed moving this date and was still moving through the legislature when this page was written. Whether it held is the first thing to check before you budget for conformity work.

  28. You are here September 2026

    Everything above is in force or decided. Everything below is on the calendar and could move.

  29. Jan 12, 2027

    Data Act: cloud switching charges end scheduled

    From this date a customer leaving your cloud service cannot be charged for the switch. Egress fees in your pricing need a plan before then.

  30. Aug 2, 2027

    AI Act: high-risk rules for AI inside regulated products scheduled

    Medical devices, machinery, vehicles, and the other product categories with their own EU safety law. The omnibus would move this one too.

Where you stand, September 2026

What applies to you today

  • The GDPR in full, including a representative in the EU if you have no entity there and a data processing agreement with every customer who asks.
  • Cookie consent under each country's version of the ePrivacy rule, to the Planet49 standard: a real choice, no pre-ticked boxes.
  • Transfers to the US through the Data Privacy Framework for vendors that certified, or the 2021 clauses plus an assessment for those that did not.
  • The AI Act's prohibited practices and general-purpose model duties, and whatever the high-risk timetable turned out to be.
  • The Data Act, if you run a connected product or a cloud service: data access on request and a working way out.

Watch next

  • The Digital Omnibus, article by article. The personal-data definition and the cookie change would each remove work; neither is law until adopted.
  • The AI Act's high-risk date. If it moved, the new one; if it held, whether any of your features are in Annex III.
  • The Data Privacy Framework challenge. A third invalidation would send every US vendor back to clauses, so keep the assessment templates.
  • January 12, 2027, if any of your revenue is egress or switching fees.
