From opening the role to day one: a senior engineer hire, week by week

Week 1 · Aug 31

Open the role

  • Our side
  • Role doc and scorecard written: what the first year has to produce, and what we will ask in each session. HM
  • Posted, and the first thirty sourced names sent a personal note. Rec
  • Panel of four confirmed and calibrated on the scorecard in one hour. HM
  • The candidate
  • Sees the posting, or gets a note that names the team and the problem, not a template.
  • Applies. Hears back within three business days either way.

Week 2 · Sep 7

Screen Now

  • Our side
  • Thirty-minute screens, Monday to Thursday. Rec
  • Shortlist reviewed Friday morning; passes and declines sent the same day. HM
  • Decision. Who goes to the technical round.
  • The candidate
  • One thirty-minute call: the role, the compensation band, and the process with its dates.
  • Told yes or no by Friday. Nobody is left waiting over a weekend.

Week 3 · Sep 14

Technical

  • Our side
  • One hour pairing on a real problem from our codebase with a senior engineer. No take-home. Panel
  • Written feedback against the scorecard the same day. Panel
  • The candidate
  • A prep note the week before: the problem area, the language, and what we look for.
  • Hears within two business days.

Week 4 · Sep 21

The loop

  • Our side
  • Four sessions in one day: architecture, a past project in depth, working with others, product sense. Panel
  • Debrief the same afternoon, scorecards in before anyone talks. HM
  • Decision. Hire or not, that day.
  • The candidate
  • Half a day, remote, with a real break. Meets the manager and two future teammates.
  • A decision within three business days, with reasons if it is a no.

Week 5 · Sep 28

Offer

  • Our side
  • Number set from the band, not negotiated up from a guess. Fin
  • Two references, with the candidate's consent. Rec
  • Offer call, then the letter the same day. HM
  • Decision. Theirs. Accepted sets the start date.
  • The candidate
  • Hears the number on a call before it arrives in writing.
  • A week to decide. Questions go to the manager, not the recruiter.

Weeks 6-9 · Oct 5

Notice

  • Our side
  • Laptop ordered and accounts created in the first week, not the last. IT
  • Buddy assigned; the first-week plan written with a first task in it. Buddy
  • A short reading list and one hello from the team. HM
  • The candidate
  • Gives notice. Gets the welcome note, the reading list, and the buddy's name.
  • Nothing is required of them. The list is there if they want it.

Week 10 · Nov 2

Day one

  • Our side
  • Buddy meets them at nine. Setup done by lunch because IT did it in week six. Buddy
  • A first small change shipped inside the first week, chosen in advance. HM
  • The candidate
  • A schedule for the day, one meeting with the manager, and a first task with a clear finish line.
  • Knows who to ask, and that asking is expected.

Three decisions set the pace

Everything else in the plan is work that can slip a day without anyone noticing. These three are the moments the candidate is waiting on, and where the last hires lost time.

  1. Friday, week 2

    The shortlist

    Hiring manager decides who goes to the technical round. Every day this waits is a week lost, because the panel's calendars are booked from it.

  2. Loop day, week 4

    Hire or not

    The panel scores, the hiring manager decides, the same afternoon. A debrief that slides to the next week is where two of the last three senior hires nearly went elsewhere.

  3. Week 5, theirs

    Accepted

    The candidate's decision, and the only date on this page we do not control. It sets day one, and with it IT's clock and the buddy's.
