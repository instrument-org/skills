Itinerary · Lisbon

Four slow days in Lisbon with a two-year-old

Thursday to Sunday from an apartment in Alfama, built around one outing each morning, a real nap, and one easy outing after. The hills are the plan's enemy, so every day starts from the door on foot and comes home by taxi.

Days
Four, Thursday to Sunday
Arrive Wednesday night, leave Monday morning
Base
Alfama, near Portas do Sol
Two viewpoints and the castle within ten minutes on foot
Pace
Slow
One morning outing, a nap window, one afternoon outing, dinner at opening
Who
Two adults, one two-year-old
Carrier for the hills, stroller for the flat days

The four days

Day 1 · Thursday

On foot, carrier

Alfama on foot

  1. Morning

    Miradouro de Santa Luzia, then down through the lanes

    Five minutes from the door. Tiled benches, the river, and the trams turning below, which is the whole show for a two-year-old. Wander down past the Sé to Campo das Cebolas, where there is a small playground and flat ground. Lunch at a tasca on the square around noon.

  2. Nap

    Roughly 1 to 3, back at the apartment. Taxi up the hill from the square; it is a few minutes and beats the stairs with a sleeping child.

  3. Afternoon

    Castelo de São Jorge Book ahead

    Ten minutes uphill from the base. Peacocks, ramparts to walk, and lawns to run on; an hour and a half is plenty. Carrier, not stroller: the ramparts are steps.

  4. Evening

    Dinner near Largo do Chafariz de Dentro

    Restaurants open for dinner around 7; be at the door when they do. Grilled fish, a high chair if you ask, and home before the fado starts.

Day 2 · Friday

Taxi out, stroller all day

Belém, flat and by the river

  1. Morning

    Mosteiro dos Jerónimos, then Pastéis de Belém Book ahead

    Taxi at 9 for the first cloister slot, before the coach groups. The cloisters are stroller-friendly and an hour is enough. Cross to the pastry shop for the custard tarts, then the lawns of the Praça do Império for the child to run.

  2. Nap

    In the stroller. Walk the riverfront path west toward the tower and back; it is flat, shaded in stretches, and the motion does the work. Lunch at a kiosk on the way.

  3. Afternoon

    Torre de Belém from the outside, and the MAAT lawn

    Skip the tower's interior; the spiral stair is not for a carrier. The museum's roof is a grass slope a toddler will climb for an hour. Taxi home around 4.

  4. Evening

    Playground at the Santa Clara garden, dinner in Graça

    The garden on Campo de Santa Clara is up the hill behind the base and has the closest good playground. Dinner at one of the tascas on the way back down.

Day 3 · Saturday

Metro, stroller all day

The aquarium and the waterfront

  1. Morning

    Oceanário de Lisboa Book ahead

    Metro from Santa Apolónia to the Parque das Nações station, elevators at both ends. Book the opening slot; the central tank is at toddler eye level on the lower floor and the otters are the hit. Two hours, then lunch at the Vasco da Gama mall next door, which has changing rooms.

  2. Nap

    In the stroller along the Parque das Nações promenade, which is the flattest mile in the city. The cable car runs above it if the child is awake and wants to see the river from the sky.

  3. Afternoon

    Jardins Garcia de Orta and the playgrounds

    The gardens run along the promenade with several playgrounds and a water feature. Stay an hour, then Metro home.

  4. Evening

    Miradouro da Graça at sunset

    Taxi up. A kiosk sells drinks and snacks, the terrace is walled, and the view is the castle and the whole city going orange. Dinner nearby, then downhill to bed.

Day 4 · Sunday

Tram out, taxi home

A tram ride and a garden

  1. Morning

    Tram 28 to Estrela, early

    Board at Graça before 9 with the stroller folded; later than that it is standing room. The child sits at the window for the whole rattle across town. Get off at Estrela for the Jardim da Estrela: a big playground, a duck pond, and a kiosk cafe for a long late breakfast.

  2. Nap

    Roughly 1 to 3, back at the apartment. Taxi from the garden gate; do not attempt the 28 back with a sleeping child.

  3. Afternoon

    Museu Nacional do Azulejo Confirm hours

    A short taxi east of the base. Quiet, mostly flat, a cloister garden with a cafe, and tiles of animals at knee height. An hour, no more. Or skip it and let the afternoon be nothing.

  4. Evening

    Last dinner back at Campo das Cebolas

    Return to the square from Day 1, since the child already knows the playground. Pack after bedtime.

Stroller, hills, and what to book

Getting around

  • Alfama is stairs and cobbles: carrier inside the neighborhood and at the castle, stroller for Belém, the waterfront, and Estrela.
  • A taxi or a ride app up the hill costs a few euros and is the plan's secret. Tram 28 is for one early ride, not for transport.
  • The Metro has elevators at Santa Apolónia and at the Parque das Nações station. Buy a rechargeable transit card at the first station; the child rides free.

Book ahead

  • Jerónimos cloisters. Timed entry online; take the first slot of the day.
  • Oceanário. Timed entry online; the opening slot is the quietest.
  • Castelo. Buy online the night before to skip the gate line.
  • Under-threes are free at all three.

Carry

  • Sun hat and a light rain layer; the weather turns at the river.
  • Snacks and a water bottle, since lunch is late here and a hungry toddler at 12:30 is the day's biggest risk.
  • A folding stroller that fits a taxi trunk, and shoes with grip for the adults: the cobbles are polished.

What costs money

No budget was set, so no figures here. The priced tickets are the castle, the monastery cloisters, and the aquarium, and the child is free at each. Everything else on the page is a viewpoint, a garden, a tram or Metro fare, a taxi up a hill, or a tasca lunch, which is the cheap end of Western Europe. The aquarium is the one ticket that costs real money for two adults.

If it rains, or everyone is tired

  • Rain on Day 1 or 4. The Museu do Fado is two minutes from the base and small enough for a toddler's attention; the Sé is dry and echoes.
  • Rain on Day 2. Swap Belém for Day 3's aquarium and do Belém on the first dry day; the cloisters are half outdoors.
  • Rain on Day 3. The aquarium is already indoors. Add the Pavilhão do Conhecimento next door, a science center with a room for the under-sixes.
  • Tired. Drop the afternoon block, keep the nap and the evening. The evenings are the part the child will remember.

The places, once

  • D1Miradouro de Santa Luzia. Viewpoint with tiled benches, next to Portas do Sol.
  • D1Sé de Lisboa. The cathedral, on the way down the hill.
  • D1Campo das Cebolas. Riverside square with a small playground and tascas.
  • D1Castelo de São Jorge. The castle above Alfama; ramparts, peacocks, lawns.
  • D2Mosteiro dos Jerónimos. Monastery in Belém; the cloisters are the visit.
  • D2Pastéis de Belém. The custard tart bakery across the road from the monastery.
  • D2Torre de Belém. Riverside tower, seen from the path; skip the interior.
  • D2MAAT. Art and architecture museum with a grass roof to climb.
  • D2Campo de Santa Clara garden. Garden and playground on the hill behind the base.
  • D3Oceanário de Lisboa. Aquarium at Parque das Nações.
  • D3Parque das Nações. Flat waterfront district with a promenade, gardens, and a cable car.
  • D3Miradouro da Graça. Walled viewpoint with a kiosk, facing the castle.
  • D4Tram 28. The yellow tram across the old city; ride it once, early.
  • D4Jardim da Estrela. Large garden with a playground, a pond, and a kiosk cafe.
  • D4Museu Nacional do Azulejo. Tile museum in a convent, with a garden cafe.
  • RainMuseu do Fado, Pavilhão do Conhecimento. The fado museum by the base; the science center by the aquarium.
