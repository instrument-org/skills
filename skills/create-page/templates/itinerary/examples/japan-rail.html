Ten days in Japan by rail: Tokyo, a night in Hakone, Kyoto, Osaka

A first trip, in one direction, with four nights in Tokyo, one in a ryokan with an onsen, three in Kyoto, and two in Osaka. Four train legs, all reserved seats, and the big suitcases sent ahead once so the onsen night is carried in a backpack.

Days
Ten, arriving Day 1, flying out Day 10
Bases
Shinjuku 4, Hakone 1, Kyoto 3, Osaka 2
Pace
Brisk mornings, one district per afternoon, early dinners
Who
First-timers who walk all day and want the trains to be part of it

  1. Tokyo · four nights

    Base in Shinjuku, west side, for the Odakyu line out to Hakone and a Yamanote stop on the doorstep.

  2. Day 1

    Arrive and stay awake until dark

    Afternoon
    Airport to Shinjuku: the Narita Express from Narita (about 80 min) or the Keikyu line from Haneda (about 40 min, one change). Add a Suica to the phone wallet before landing; it pays for every train, bus, and convenience store from here on.
    Evening
    Check in, then Omoide Yokocho for a first bowl and a counter seat under the tracks. Walk the Kabukicho neon once, sleep by 9.

  3. Day 2

    Tokyo, the east side

    Morning
    Senso-ji in Asakusa by 7, while the jet lag is useful and Nakamise is still shuttered. Then Kappabashi, the kitchen street, for a knife or a pair of chopsticks to carry home.
    Afternoon
    Walk or ride one stop to Ueno: the park, and one museum of the four (the Tokyo National Museum is the one to choose the first time). Ameyoko market for a standing snack.
    Evening
    Akihabara after dark, for the lights and one retro game floor. Dinner: a curry counter in the station.

  4. Day 3

    Tokyo, the west side

    Morning
    Meiji Jingu at opening, through the cedar avenue from Harajuku station. Then Omotesando's architecture walk down to Aoyama.
    Afternoon
    Shibuya: the crossing from the station's second floor, the Hachiko exit, and a wander up to Miyashita Park. Shibuya Sky at the sunset slot. Book ahead · weeks
    Evening
    Yakitori in Ebisu, two stops south, where the queues are shorter than Shibuya's.

  5. Day 4

    Tokyo, the bay, and the suitcases go ahead

    Morning
    Before leaving the hotel, hand the big suitcases to the front desk for luggage forwarding to the Kyoto hotel; they arrive the next day. Pack an overnight bag for Hakone. Then teamLab Planets in Toyosu for the opening slot. Book ahead · weeks
    Afternoon
    Tsukiji outer market for a late lunch of whatever is grilled at the stall with the shortest line, then Ginza on foot: the department store basements and the stationery floors.
    Evening
    Yurakucho, under the railway arches, for the last Tokyo dinner. Early night; tomorrow's train is a morning one.

  6. Shinjuku → Hakone-YumotoOdakyu Romancecarabout 1 h 25Reserve the seat

  7. Hakone · one night

    A ryokan with its own bath, in the hills above the Hakone-Yumoto station. This is the onsen night, so the ryokan is the day.

  8. Day 5

    The Hakone loop, then the bath

    Morning
    Leave the overnight bag at the station lockers or the ryokan's desk. Buy the Hakone Free Pass at Shinjuku with the train ticket; it covers the whole loop. Tozan railway up the switchbacks, cable car, then the ropeway over Owakudani's steaming vents.
    Afternoon
    The ropeway down to Lake Ashi, the boat across to Moto-Hakone, the cedar avenue and the shrine gate in the water. Bus back to Yumoto to check in by 3. Ryokan · months
    Evening
    Bath before dinner, kaiseki in the room or the dining hall at the hour the ryokan sets, bath again, futon. Nothing else is planned, on purpose.

  9. Hakone-Yumoto → Odawara → KyotoTozan line, then Hikari shinkansenabout 15 min, then about 2 hReserve the seat

  10. Kyoto · three nights

    Base near Karasuma-Oike, mid-city, so both the east and west sides are one bus or subway ride.

  11. Day 6

    Arrive Kyoto, the center on foot

    Morning
    On the train. Sit on the right side out of Odawara for Mount Fuji, weather permitting, in the first half hour.
    Afternoon
    Drop bags, collect the forwarded suitcases, then Nishiki market end to end and the covered arcades north of it. Tea at a kissaten.
    Evening
    Gion at dusk: Hanamikoji, Yasaka shrine lit up, then dinner along Pontocho's alley by the river.

  12. Day 7

    Kyoto, the eastern hills

    Morning
    Fushimi Inari by 7, JR from Kyoto station, two stops. Climb past the crowds to the Yotsutsuji crossing, about 45 minutes up, and come down the quieter side. Then Tofuku-ji's garden on the way back.
    Afternoon
    Bus to Kiyomizu-dera, then downhill through Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka to Kodai-ji and Maruyama park. This is the crowded stretch; it is worth it once.
    Evening
    Okonomiyaki near the base, then an early bath at a neighborhood sento if the legs want it.

  13. Day 8

    Kyoto, west to Arashiyama and the gold pavilion

    Morning
    JR Sagano line to Saga-Arashiyama, in the bamboo grove by 8. Tenryu-ji's garden, then the Togetsukyo bridge and a river walk. Lunch of yudofu in the district.
    Afternoon
    Bus across to Kinkaku-ji, then Ryoan-ji's rock garden fifteen minutes' walk away. Two temples, no more; the third is the one that blurs.
    Evening
    Kyoto station's ramen floor, and the rooftop skyway for a free look at the city. Pack the suitcases for a short hop.

  14. Kyoto → OsakaJR special rapidabout 30 minno reservation, tap in with Suica

  15. Osaka · two nights

    Base in Namba, so Dotonbori is on foot and the airport train leaves from the doorstep.

  16. Day 9

    Osaka, the castle and the street food

    Morning
    Drop bags in Namba, then subway to Osaka Castle: the park and the moat walls are the point, the reconstructed keep is optional. Kuromon market for lunch on the way back.
    Afternoon
    Shinsekai, the old amusement district under Tsutenkaku tower, for kushikatsu at a counter. Or Nakanoshima's riverside and the library if the group wants quiet.
    Evening
    Dotonbori at full volume: takoyaki from a stall, the running man sign, and a walk along the canal. Then Hozenji Yokocho's lantern alley, one street over and silent.

  17. Day 10

    A last morning, then the airport

    Morning
    Umeda Sky Building's open-air roof for one last look, or a depachika breakfast at the Hankyu food hall beneath it. Back to Namba for the bags.
    Afternoon
    Nankai Rapi:t from Namba to Kansai airport, about 40 min, reserved seat bought at the counter that morning. If the flight home leaves from Tokyo instead, the Nozomi from Shin-Osaka takes about 2 h 30 and this becomes a travel day.
    Evening
    In the air.

Getting around

  • Skip the national rail pass. Since its price rose, three one-way legs cost less than seven days of pass. Buy the shinkansen seats in the smartEX app a few days ahead and pick up nothing.
  • Suica on the phone for every local train, bus, and subway in all three cities, and for the lockers.
  • Luggage forwarding from the Tokyo hotel to the Kyoto hotel on Day 4, so the Hakone night is a backpack.

Book ahead, in this order

  • The ryokan. Months ahead; the ones with private baths go first.
  • teamLab Planets, Shibuya Sky. Weeks ahead, timed slots.
  • Romancecar and shinkansen seats. Days ahead, in the apps.
  • Restaurants. None on this page need it; a counter seat is the plan.

Carry

  • Some cash for shrines, markets, and small counters; the phone handles the rest.
  • Shoes that slip off, for temples and the ryokan. Socks without holes.
  • A small towel and a coin purse, and a folding bag for the Kappabashi haul.

What drives the cost

No budget was given, so no figures. The ryokan night with dinner and breakfast is the single largest line and the one worth it. The three reserved train legs are the second. Everything else scales with how the group eats: a counter lunch is cheap, and the department store basement is where the money goes if it goes.

Rain, or a tired day

  • Tokyo. Swap a day for the Tokyo National Museum and the Ginza basements; Day 4 is already half indoors.
  • Hakone. The ropeway closes in high wind and the mountain hides in cloud. Do the Hakone Open-Air Museum on the Tozan line instead, then check in early. The bath is the point either way.
  • Kyoto. Rain suits the temples; keep the day and carry an umbrella. If tired, cut the afternoon temples and sit in a kissaten.
  • Osaka. The aquarium at Kaiyukan replaces the castle.

The places, once

  • D1Omoide Yokocho. Alley of yakitori counters by Shinjuku station.
  • D2Senso-ji. Asakusa's temple and its market street.
  • D2Kappabashi. The restaurant supply street; knives and plastic food.
  • D2Ueno Park, Tokyo National Museum, Ameyoko. Park, museum, and market in one walk.
  • D2Akihabara. Electronics and arcades.
  • D3Meiji Jingu, Omotesando. Forest shrine, then the avenue of flagship buildings.
  • D3Shibuya Sky. Open rooftop above the crossing; timed entry.
  • D4teamLab Planets. Immersive art in Toyosu; barefoot, timed entry.
  • D4Tsukiji outer market, Ginza, Yurakucho. Lunch stalls, department stores, dinner under the arches.
  • D5The Hakone loop. Tozan railway, cable car, ropeway over Owakudani, boat on Lake Ashi, bus back.
  • D6Nishiki market, Gion, Pontocho. Food street, the geisha district, the dinner alley.
  • D7Fushimi Inari, Tofuku-ji. The torii mountain and a garden temple, both by JR.
  • D7Kiyomizu-dera, Sannenzaka, Kodai-ji. The hillside temple and the lanes below it.
  • D8Arashiyama, Tenryu-ji. Bamboo grove, garden temple, river.
  • D8Kinkaku-ji, Ryoan-ji. The gold pavilion and the rock garden.
  • D9Osaka Castle, Kuromon, Shinsekai, Dotonbori. Castle park, market, old amusement quarter, the canal of signs.
  • D10Umeda Sky Building. Open-air roof over Osaka; food hall beneath.
  • RainHakone Open-Air Museum, Kaiyukan. Sculpture park on the Tozan line; Osaka's aquarium.
