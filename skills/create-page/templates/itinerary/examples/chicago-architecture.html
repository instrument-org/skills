Itinerary · Chicago

A long weekend in Chicago, for two people who read buildings

Friday evening to Monday morning, on foot and by the L, with the river cruise as the spine and three Wright houses as the reason to leave the Loop. Each day is a walk in one direction; the places beside it are in the order you will reach them.

Days

Friday evening to Monday morning

Two full days, two half days

Base

The Loop, near the river

So the cruise dock and every L line are a walk

Pace

Long walks, few tickets

Lobbies and facades most of the day, one tour each afternoon

Who

Two adults who will stop for a cornice

Comfortable shoes, no car

Friday

Arrive, and the Loop at golden hour

  1. 1Chicago Architecture Center. Galleries and the city model, on the river at Michigan Avenue.
  2. 2Michigan Avenue streetwall. The row of facades facing Grant Park, 1880s to 1920s.
  3. 3Chicago Cultural Center. Free; the Tiffany dome upstairs.
  4. 4Millennium Park. Gehry's Pritzker Pavilion and its bridge, the Bean.
  5. 5Art Institute, Modern Wing. Renzo Piano, seen from the park side.

Arrive, and the Loop at golden hour

Afternoon. Check in, then walk to the Chicago Architecture Center for an hour with the model of the city before you have seen any of it; it makes the next two days legible. Book the Saturday river cruise here if it is not already booked. Cross the bridge and walk south along the Michigan Avenue streetwall, reading the row from the park side of the street, where the whole cliff is visible at once.

Late afternoon. Into the Cultural Center for the dome, free and quiet, then across to Millennium Park as the light drops: the pavilion's steel from underneath, the serpentine bridge, and the Modern Wing's flying roof from the park side. Sit on the lawn until the towers light.

Dinner in the West Loop, then the Riverwalk back east under the lit bridges.

Saturday

The river, then the Loop by lobby

  1. 1Architecture river cruise. From the dock below Michigan Avenue; about 90 minutes. Book ahead
  2. 2Marina City. Goldberg's corncobs, 1964, from the north bank.
  3. 3Reliance Building. 1895, the glass-and-terracotta ancestor of every curtain wall; now a hotel.
  4. 4Sullivan Center. Sullivan's cast-iron ornament over the corner entrance, 1899.
  5. 5Marquette Building. Lobby mosaics, 1895. Confirm weekend hours
  6. 6Federal Center. Mies's plaza, the post office pavilion, the Calder.
  7. 7Monadnock Building. 1891, the last tall wall-bearing brick building, and its later steel half.
  8. 8The Rookery. 1888, Burnham and Root; Wright's 1905 lobby. Confirm weekend hours
  9. 9Chicago Board of Trade. Holabird and Root's art deco, closing LaSalle Street.
  10. 10Aqua and St. Regis. Studio Gang's two towers, from the Riverwalk or Lakeshore East park.

The river, then the Loop by lobby

Morning. The first cruise of the day, when the light is on the east faces and the boat is half empty. It is the best ninety minutes of architecture talk in the country and it settles the argument about which tower is which. Step off and look back at Marina City from the bank, then walk south into the Loop.

Afternoon. The Loop in the order the places list gives, which runs south down State and back north up LaSalle in about two miles. Lobbies are the point: the Reliance's, the Marquette's mosaics, and the Rookery's light court, which Wright whitened in 1905. Some Loop lobbies keep office hours and close on weekends, so the two tagged above are the ones to confirm and to swap for Monday if they are shut. Finish at the Board of Trade, then cut east along the river to see the two Gang towers from below.

Dinner in River North; a drink on the Athletic Association roof, looking down the park at the streetwall you walked yesterday.

Sunday

Wright, west and south

  1. 1Wright Home and Studio. Oak Park, 1889 and after; guided tour. Book ahead
  2. 2Forest Avenue houses. The Moore, Heurtley, and Thomas houses, exteriors only, on the streets around the studio.
  3. 3Unity Temple. 1908, concrete, the sanctuary lit from above. Book ahead
  4. 4Robie House. Hyde Park, 1910, the Prairie house. Book ahead
  5. 5University of Chicago quads. Collegiate Gothic, Rockefeller Chapel, and Jahn's glass Mansueto Library dome.
  6. 6Promontory Point. The lakefront park, with the skyline five miles north.

Wright, west and south

Morning. Green Line west to Oak Park, about half an hour from the Loop, for the first tour of the Home and Studio. Then the self-guided walk down Forest Avenue, where the houses go from Queen Anne to Prairie in three blocks, and on to Unity Temple for its tour. Lunch on Lake Street in Oak Park before heading back.

Afternoon. Back through the Loop and south to Hyde Park by Metra Electric from Millennium station, about 20 minutes, for the Robie House tour. Walk the quads afterward: the Gothic core, the chapel, and the library dome, which is the day's one building after 1950. End at the lake.

Dinner in Hyde Park, then the lakefront path at Promontory Point until the skyline lights.

Monday, before the flight

Mies, then the airport

  1. 1Crown Hall. Mies, 1956, on the Illinois Institute of Technology campus; Koolhaas's student center wraps the L beside it.
  2. 2860 and 880 Lake Shore Drive. Mies's 1951 towers, if the flight is late enough for a detour north.

Mies, then the airport

Morning. Bags at the hotel desk, then the Green Line to the Illinois Institute of Technology for Crown Hall, one room of glass and steel that explains half the Loop's later towers. The campus is a fifteen-minute wander. Either back to the Loop for the bags and the Orange Line to Midway, or, with a later flight, the Red Line north for the two lakefront towers and a taxi from there.

Lunch is the airport's.

Getting around

  • A Ventra card or a tapped phone covers the L and buses. Metra Electric to Hyde Park is a separate ticket, bought in its app.
  • The Loop walk on Saturday is about two miles; Oak Park's houses are within a mile of the station.
  • Every day ends near an L stop on purpose, so no taxi is planned.

Book ahead

  • The river cruise. Weeks ahead in summer; the first departure of the day.
  • Wright Home and Studio, Unity Temple, Robie House. Timed tours, a week or two ahead, and the three together fit one Sunday only if the first is the earliest slot.
  • The rooftop bar. A reservation saves the wait.

Carry

  • A wind layer at any time of year; the river and the lake make their own weather.
  • Shoes for two miles of terrazzo and a mile of sidewalk.
  • Binoculars for cornices, and no tripod: most lobbies allow a phone and nothing more.

What costs money

No budget was given, so no figures. The priced items are the cruise and the three house tours; the Loop, Oak Park's streets, the campus, and the lakefront cost a train fare. The rooftop drink is the one discretionary line.

If it rains, or the legs give out

  • Saturday. The cruise runs in rain under a canopy. Move the Loop walk underground through the Pedway between lobbies, and put the Art Institute's interiors in place of the LaSalle Street half.
  • Sunday. The tours are indoors. Cut the Forest Avenue walk and the quads; keep the three houses.
  • Tired. Drop Hyde Park entirely and give Oak Park the whole day; Robie House is the loss, and it is the smallest of the three.

The places

Every place on this page is in the rail beside its day, numbered in walking order, with its architect and date where they are the reason to go. Nothing appears twice, so the rails together are the list.
