ops-console v2 · owner: platform · decision: this week

Rebuild the ops dashboard on the warehouse

Replace the Rails admin pages that ops reads from production Postgres with a read-only console on the warehouse finance already uses, so ops stops exporting CSVs and production stops paying for their queries.

Problem

The ops dashboard is a set of admin pages bolted onto the production app in 2022. The orders view takes about 40 s to load at peak because its aggregates run against the primary, and those queries are the top source of the slow-query alerts on-call gets paged for. Ops works around it by exporting CSVs into spreadsheets: five people, roughly five hours a week each, rebuilding views the dashboard should show. Finance reads the warehouse, so ops and finance reconcile their numbers by hand at month end.

before

  • Admin pages inside the production Rails app.
  • Aggregates run against the primary database.
  • About 40 s for the orders view at peak; on-call paged on slow queries.
  • CSV export, then a spreadsheet, for anything past the built-in views.
  • Numbers differ from finance until reconciled by hand.

after

  • A standalone read-only console with its own deploy.
  • Reads the warehouse; three live views read a replica. Nothing touches the primary.
  • Views load in a second or two; each shows its data's freshness.
  • Saved filters and column pickers replace the exports.
  • Same tables finance reads, so the numbers match by construction.

Why now

The warehouse now lands orders, refunds, and fulfillment events within fifteen minutes, which it did not a year ago, so a read-only console no longer needs a second pipeline. The platform team has a quiet four weeks after the launch freeze lifts and nothing scheduled against them. And the slow-query alert has paged three of the last four on-call rotations.

Costs, in engineer-weeks

  • 8 engineer-weeks to build: two engineers, four weeks, on the console and its views.
  • 2 engineer-weeks to cut over: a parallel run, the view sign-off with ops, removal of the old pages.
  • About a day a month to keep, since the warehouse team owns the pipelines and the console owns only its views.

Returns, in hours a week

  • About 25 hours a week back to ops: five people at roughly five hours each of exports and spreadsheet rebuilds, by their own tally.
  • Slow-query pages from the admin pages go to zero, since nothing reads the primary.
  • The month-end reconciliation between ops and finance goes away; both read the same tables.

The ask

Approve two engineers for four weeks, starting the Monday after the freeze lifts.

A yes this week keeps the quiet window; a yes next month runs into the next quarter's roadmap.

  • The two engineers, the platform team's pick.
  • Read access to the warehouse's ops schema for the console's service account.
  • The ops lead's sign-off on the view list, which she has already drafted.

Risks, with a mitigation each

  • The warehouse lags production by up to fifteen minutes.

    fix Queue depth, failed jobs, and refund holds stay live on a read replica; every other view tolerates the lag and shows its freshness.

  • Scope grows into a full admin rewrite.

    fix v1 is read-only. Refunds and manual overrides stay in the old admin until a v2 is approved on its own.

  • Two engineers off the roadmap for a month.

    fix Scheduled in the post-freeze quiet weeks; the platform hardening sprint slips two weeks and nothing customer-facing moves.

  • Ops keeps using the old pages.

    fix Old pages go read-only at cutover and are removed after thirty days; the ops lead signed the view list.

Next step. If yes: kickoff the Monday after the freeze lifts, the first three views to ops at the end of week 2, parallel run in week 4, old pages read-only the Monday after.
