One-pager · For the community center board

The Fairmount Tool Library

A lending shelf of hand and power tools in the center's east room, open two afternoons a week and run by neighbors, so a household can borrow a drill for a weekend instead of buying one for a lifetime.

The problem

Most houses on these blocks own one tool they use twice a year and lack the one they need this weekend. A porch repair waits on a ladder, a garden waits on a tiller, and the nearest hardware store is a bus ride away. Renting a tiller for a Saturday costs more than the seedlings, so the garden does not happen. Neighbors already lend to each other; they just have no way to know who has what.

The proposal

A tool library in the center's east storage room: shelving, a lockable cabinet, a sign-out sheet, and a small annual membership. Open Tuesday and Saturday afternoons, staffed by two volunteers a shift from a crew of eight. Tools come from donations first, then a starter purchase with the neighborhood association's seed money. Hand tools go out for a week, power tools for a weekend, with a deposit on the expensive ones.

Why now

The east room has been empty since the after-school program moved upstairs. The association's seed money has to be spent by the end of its fiscal year in June. And spring is when people fix things: a March opening catches the first porch and garden season, and a fall opening waits a year for the same neighbors.

What it costs the center

  • The east room, two afternoons a week, for a one-year trial.
  • A rider on the center's insurance, to be quoted before the first loan.
  • Shelving and a lockable cabinet, one time, paid from the seed money rather than the center's budget.
  • A place on the center's website and bulletin board.

What it gives back

  • Households borrow the ladder, the drill, the tiller, and the wet vac instead of buying or going without.
  • Foot traffic on the center's quietest afternoons, from people who have never come in.
  • Membership fees cover repairs and replacements after the first year, so keeping it costs the center nothing.
  • A volunteer crew of eight that knows the building and shows up twice a week.

The ask

Three things the board can say yes to at its November meeting

  1. 1 The room. The east storage room on Tuesday and Saturday afternoons, for a one-year trial starting in March.
  2. 2 The paperwork. Add the library to the center's insurance, and let members sign up on the center's own membership form so there is one list, not two.
  3. 3 The name. Let us call it the Fairmount Community Center Tool Library, so a neighbor trusts it on the first day.

What could go wrong, honestly

  • Someone gets hurt with a borrowed tool. Members sign a waiver, every power tool goes out with a one-page safety sheet, and nothing lends until the insurance rider is in place.
  • Tools do not come back. A deposit on anything expensive, a reminder call at the due date, and a replacement line in the budget, because a few will walk and we would rather plan for it than pretend.
  • The volunteers get tired. Two shifts a week, a rotation of eight, and a trial year that ends with a review in front of this board instead of a quiet fade.
  • Nobody comes. If fewer than a few dozen households have joined by the end of summer, we hand the room back and the tools become the center's.

Next step. If the board says yes in November, we hold a donation day in January, get the insurance quote in February, and open on the first Saturday in March with whatever the neighborhood has given by then.

Provenance. Prepared by the Fairmount neighborhood association's tool library committee for the community center board.
