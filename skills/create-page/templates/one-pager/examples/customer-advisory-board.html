A customer advisory board for Meridian

Twelve paying customers, four sessions a year, one owner: a standing group that sees the roadmap two quarters early and tells us what to build before we commit engineers to it.

Problem

Roadmap bets are made on the loudest signal in the room. Two of the last four quarterly bets traced back to a single sales call, and we learned what our largest accounts wanted at their renewal, after the quarter was planned. Support tickets say what broke; nothing says what customers plan to build next year.

Proposal

Twelve customers, four seats per segment (mid-market, enterprise, partners), meeting for ninety minutes each quarter on video and for one day a year in person. Product runs it, an exec sponsors it. Every session previews the roadmap two quarters out under NDA and brings one open decision, with options, for the board to argue over.

Outcome

Each quarterly bet is argued over by the accounts that pay for it before an engineer is assigned. Launches ship with a named reference customer. Renewal talks with twelve of our largest accounts start from a relationship, not a QBR slide.

Why now

Annual planning opens in November and the roadmap locks in December. A first session in late October is the only way to put customer voices into next year's plan rather than the one after. Two enterprise renewals this spring cited "no say in the roadmap" in the account team's notes, and the team has been promising a board since then with nothing to point to.

What it costs

  • One product manager, about a day a week for the first quarter, less after.
  • The exec sponsor, two hours a quarter plus the in-person day.
  • The in-person day for twelve, from Marketing's events line.
  • One NDA template from Legal, reused for every member.

What it returns

  • Every quarterly bet read by paying customers before engineering commits.
  • A reference customer for each launch, from members who saw it early.
  • Earlier warning on renewals at risk, from people in the room every quarter.
  • A design-partner pool for betas, without recruiting one each time.

The ask

Approve the board by October 9, before annual planning opens.

One decision in three parts, so the first session lands in late October.

  • A yes on the charter: twelve seats, four a segment, two-year terms.
  • An exec sponsor who commits to all four sessions a year.
  • The in-person day, funded from the events line.

Risks, and how each is handled

  • The loudest accounts steer the roadmap.

    Seats are allocated by segment, not by revenue, and every session brings one decision with options rather than an open floor.

  • Members hear a roadmap that then changes.

    Every preview is labeled a direction, not a commitment, and members hear about a change from us before they see it in a release.

  • It turns into a sales event.

    Sales attends as a guest, not a host. No pricing and no upsell in session, written into the charter.

  • It fades after two sessions.

    One named owner, four dates booked a year out, and a standing item at the quarterly product review to report what the board said.

Next step. If yes on October 9: Product sends the sponsor a shortlist of twenty candidate accounts the following week, invitations go out with the NDA by mid-October, and the first session is on the calendar before planning opens.
