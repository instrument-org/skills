How-to guide

Run a 30-minute weekly retro for a team of eight that has never done one

A playbook you can run from the page: what to have ready, what to say at each minute mark, and the one rule that keeps a first retro from turning into a complaint session. The team leaves with a single change to try, and next week's retro opens by checking whether it happened.

In the room
30 minutes, weekly
Prep, first time
20 minutes
Cost
$0, a board and sticky notes
People
8, plus you facilitating

Before the first one

  • A fixed slot on everyone's calendar, 30 minutes, the same time every week. Not the last slot on Friday, because people leave, and not right before a demo, because people prepare.
  • A board with three columns: Went well, Did not, Try. A whiteboard in the room, or a shared doc or board with those three headings for a remote team.
  • Sticky notes and markers, one pad per person, or the digital equivalent. One thought per note is the whole format.
  • A timer everyone can see. A phone propped on the table or a timer tab shared on screen. The minute marks below only work if they are visible.
  • The message on the right, sent the day before. A team that has never done a retro assumes it is a performance review. One paragraph fixes that.

Send the day before

"Starting Thursday at 2, we'll spend 30 minutes a week looking at how the week went, not what we shipped. Three columns: went well, didn't, try. Everyone writes, I read, we pick one thing to try the following week. Nobody is being evaluated. Bring nothing."

The 30 minutes

  1. 0:00

    Set the frame

    3 min

    Start the timer on the minute, whoever is missing. Point at the three columns, say the rule about one thought per note, and say out loud that you will read every note without discussion first. The last sentence is the one that lets quiet people write.

    "This is thirty minutes to look at how the week went, not what we shipped, and nobody's in trouble. You'll write for seven minutes in silence, one thought per note. I'll read every note out loud before we discuss anything. We leave with exactly one thing to try next week."

  2. 0:03

    Silent writing

    7 min

    Everyone writes, including you. Notes go straight onto the board as they are written, into whichever column fits. Silence is the feature: the first person to talk sets the topic for everyone else, and the point of writing is that eight people set eight topics. If someone starts talking, smile and point at the timer.

    "Seven minutes, silent. As many notes as you want, one idea each. Stick them up as you go. Write what you'd say if nobody was listening."

    The empty-board minute

    Around minute two the board is still bare and someone jokes that nothing happened this week. Do not fill the silence and do not shorten the timer. Notes arrive in the last three minutes, once people see that others are writing.

  3. 0:10

    Read every note and cluster

    8 min

    Read each note aloud, in board order, in a flat voice, and move duplicates together as you go. No commentary, no "great point", no asking who wrote it. Eight people produce about twenty-five notes; at fifteen seconds each that is six minutes, with two spare for clustering. Name each cluster with two words on a fresh note.

    "I'll read everything. If you hear yours, you don't need to say so. We're not solving anything yet, just seeing what's here."

    Where a first retro dies

    The manager reacts to the third note, everyone watches the reaction, and the remaining notes get read to a room that is now managing the manager. If you are the manager, your job for these eight minutes is to be a text-to-speech engine. If you cannot, hand the reading to someone else before you start.

  4. 0:18

    Vote, then discuss only the winner

    7 min

    Two dots each, placed at the same time so nobody votes second. One minute to vote, then six minutes on the top cluster and nothing else. Ask what is underneath it before anyone proposes a fix; the first fix proposed in a first retro is almost always for the wrong problem. Ties go to the one in the Did not column.

    "Two dots each, on what you most want to talk about, not on your own note. Everyone up at once." Then: "Top one is code review wait times. Before anyone fixes it: what does that actually look like on a Tuesday?"

  5. 0:25

    Decide the one change

    4 min

    One action, small enough to happen by next Thursday, with one name on it and a way to tell whether it happened. Write it on a note in the Try column and read it back. If the room proposes three, ask which one they would keep if they could keep only one; that question ends the meeting on time more reliably than the timer does.

    "What's the smallest thing we could try next week that would move this? Who owns it? How will we know on Thursday whether it happened?"

    Five actions is zero actions

    A retro that ends with a list ends with nothing done, and the team learns by week three that retros produce lists. One action, every week, checked the next week, is what makes the meeting real.

  6. 0:29

    Close on the minute

    1 min

    Photograph the board, say where the action will be written, and end at 0:30 even mid-sentence. Ending on time the first week is the promise that makes people come back the second.

    "That's time. Thanks. Same slot next week, and the first thing on the board will be whether Sam's change happened."

Remote

  • The board is a shared doc or whiteboard with the three columns; everyone has it open before 0:00.
  • Everyone mutes for the 0:03 writing block. Cameras optional; a blank grid of muted faces is fine.
  • Voting is the board's own reactions or a plus-two typed by each person into chat at the same moment.

In person

  • Draw the columns before anyone arrives; the room reads an empty drawn board as "this is happening."
  • Stand at the board for 0:10; people talk to whoever is standing, so standing keeps the reading yours.
  • The photo at 0:29 is the record. Post it where the team already looks.

From week four

  • Rotate the facilitator. The 0:00 script goes to whoever runs it, and the manager writes notes like everyone else.
  • Open 0:00 with last week's action: did it happen, did it help, keep or drop. One minute, no debate.
  • Every fourth week, take 45 minutes and let the discussion cover the top two clusters.
