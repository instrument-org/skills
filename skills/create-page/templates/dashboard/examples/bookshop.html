Dashboard · Quarter to 30 September, read 6 October

A good quarter with one category quietly falling over

Takings are up 9% on the same quarter last year and up 4% on the one before, which is the headline and is not the interesting part. Underneath it, children’s books have fallen for four quarters in a row and now sit below the events line, and events — 6% of the money — brought in nearly a quarter of everyone who came in for the first time. Those two facts point the same way and neither of them shows up in the total.

The average basket is £13.18 and almost nobody spends £13. Two thirds of transactions are a single paperback under £12; the tail past £30 is 4% of transactions and 19% of the money. A quarter where the average rises can be a quarter where more people bought one book, or a quarter where fewer people bought three, and the distribution is the only place those two look different.

What is actually going wrong

Children’s has halved in a year and the shelf has not moved

£19,400 in Q3 last year, £9,900 now, down every quarter in between. It still holds the same six bays by the window, which is the warmest and most visible part of the shop. Nothing in the total says this, because fiction grew by almost exactly the amount children’s lost.

Events are 6% of the money and 22% of the new faces

£7,700 of ticket and signing revenue, against 214 of the 963 loyalty cards opened this quarter starting on an event day. Judged as a revenue line it is marginal and would be cut; judged as the top of the funnel it is the best thing in the shop. The dashboard cannot settle which of those it is, and the reason it is on the page is that the question does not otherwise get asked.

Growth is the basket, not the door

Transactions are up 2.4% and takings are up 9.1%, so roughly three quarters of the growth is people spending more rather than more people spending. That is the fragile kind: it tracks prices and the mix, and it reverses without anyone deciding anything.

The shop is illustrative. Every figure on this page was written to show what a quarterly readout looks like when it carries its own comparisons. No real business is described.

Takings are net of returns and exclude the card fee. Which is why the total here will not match a bank statement, and a readout that does not say which basis it used cannot be reconciled with anything.

A category is where a book sits on the shelf, not what the publisher calls it. Which means the boundary moves when the shop is rearranged, and the children’s decline is a shelf-space fact as much as a demand fact. Nobody rearranged those bays this year, so the comparison holds.

Every comparison is against the same quarter a year earlier, not the previous quarter, because a bookshop’s year has a shape and Q3 against Q2 mostly measures the summer.

This is a snapshot with a date on it. It was read on 6 October and it will not update. Nothing on this page is live and nothing on it is filterable: if the question needs slicing, it needs the till export and a grid, not this.
