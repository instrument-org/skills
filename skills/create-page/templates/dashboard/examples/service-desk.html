Dashboard · Service desk, quarter to 31 December

The average is twenty-one minutes and one ticket in ten waits nine hours

Every number we report on this team is a mean, and the mean is the one statistic this distribution defeats: most tickets are answered almost immediately and a long thin tail waits most of a working day. Nobody experiences twenty-one minutes. Half of the tail is one queue and most of the rest arrived between six in the evening and eight in the morning, which are both fixable and neither of which the average has ever shown.

Inside the shift the median never leaves single-digit minutes. Outside it, a ticket waits until the next morning, and 38% of the quarter’s tickets arrive outside it — the desk covers 08:00 to 18:00 and a third of the company does not.

This is the whole reason to read a distribution rather than an average. The fix is a rota question, not a performance question, and no amount of pressure on the median will find it.

Three things to act on

Stop reporting the mean

21 minutes describes nobody. The median (6 minutes) is the typical experience and the 90th percentile (9h 10m) is the one people complain about. Reporting both costs one extra column and ends an argument we have had every quarter for two years.

The out-of-hours tail is a rota, not an effort problem

38% of tickets arrive outside 08:00–18:00 and account for 71% of everything past four hours. Two hours of staggered cover at either end would move the 90th percentile more than anything the team can do inside the shift.

The backlog is old, not big

37 tickets open past a week, out of 4,318. As a share it is a rounding error and it went up by 14 anyway, which is the direction that matters. Nothing in the first-reply numbers touches this: a ticket answered in four minutes and then left for three weeks scores perfectly on every chart above.

The desk is illustrative. Every figure here was written to show what a service readout looks like when it reports a distribution instead of an average. No real team is described.

First reply means the first message from a person. Auto-acknowledgements are excluded, which is why these numbers are larger than the ones in the vendor’s own dashboard. Counting the robot would put the median at four seconds and mean nothing.

The clock runs in real time, not business time. A ticket arriving at 19:00 and answered at 08:10 waited thirteen hours, not ten minutes. Business-hours clocks make an out-of-hours problem invisible by definition, which is the problem this page is about.

Percentiles are over tickets, not over customers. One customer who filed 90 tickets is 2% of the quarter. Nothing here is weighted for that and it should be before anyone draws a conclusion about how customers experience the desk.
