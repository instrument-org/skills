Both numbers are on this page because the first is the one that arrives on the statement and the second is the one to plan with.

The line that says the pump is working

Daily energy against the day’s average outside temperature, both years.

Each dot is one day. The slope of each cloud is how much energy the house needs per degree of cold, and it is the number that does not care what the weather did: the grey year needed about 3.9 kWh for every heating degree day, this year about 1.7. That ratio of 2.3, not the bill, is the measurement.

The scatter also shows the thing a monthly bar chart hides: below about −2°C the green cloud bends upward as the pump falls back on its immersion heater. There were — such days this year. In a colder winter there would be several times as many, and the saving would be smaller than anything on this page suggests.

The house is illustrative. Every reading, price and temperature on this page was written to show what a before-and-after readout looks like when it takes its own confounder seriously. No real installation is described.

Weather correction is heating degree days on a 15.5°C base. Last year came to 2,180 and this year 1,940, which is 11% milder; the corrected saving scales this year’s use up by that ratio before comparing. It is the standard method and it is still only a correction, not a controlled experiment.

Prices are held at this year’s. Both years are priced at 24.5p per kWh of electricity and 6.4p of gas, so none of the change is the tariff moving. A comparison at each year’s own prices would show a different number and would be measuring the market rather than the house.

The standing charges are in the total and not in the per-kWh figures. Losing the gas meter removed £108 of standing charge on its own, which is 20% of the headline saving and nothing to do with how the pump performs.

One house, one year, one winter. Nothing here generalizes. It is a record of what happened at one address, and the reason for the scatter is that a slope is the only part of it that could be compared with anyone else’s.
