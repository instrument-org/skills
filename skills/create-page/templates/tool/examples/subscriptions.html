4 more descriptions appear once each, £269.60 in total. A yearly bill inside a three-month statement is indistinguishable from a one-off, so some of those are subscriptions and this page cannot tell you which.

Nothing leaves this page. What you paste is read by about eighty lines of script in your own browser and then forgotten. No request is made from this file, there is no server behind it, and it works with the network off. That is the whole reason a statement is a safe thing to put in it.

What it assumes. One charge per line, with a date and an amount somewhere on it. Two charges count as a schedule when they share a description, come back at an even interval that matches a real billing period, and no charge jumps more than a third from the one before it.

Where it is wrong. Three months cannot see a yearly bill: ROOK DOMAINS in the sample charges once and is filed as a one-off, which is exactly the charge people most want found. It will also miss a subscription whose description carries a changing reference number, and it will not know that two names are one company.

Where the sample came from. It is invented, merchants and all, and the amounts were chosen to put a price rise and a false positive in front of you. Replace the whole box with your own and the page recalculates as you paste.
