An hour that is not the middle of someone’s night

Pick the places people are in and how long you need. It draws everyone’s day across the same axis and finds the windows where nobody is asleep, ranked by how few of them are having a bad time. It arrives with five cities already in it, because a tool that opens empty is a tool you have to learn before it helps.

Who is in the meeting

Computed for Tuesday 14 April 2026, and the date matters: half the northern hemisphere has changed its clocks by then and the southern half has not, so an overlap that works in April can be an hour wrong in January. Everything runs in this page — no city list, no meeting and no calendar is sent anywhere, and the page works with the network off.

The answer it opens with

For London, New York, Los Angeles, Bengaluru and Sydney — a set with nineteen hours between its ends — there is no two-hour window in which all five are inside a normal working day, and the tool says so rather than picking the least bad one and calling it a result. The best it can do is 15:00 to 17:00 UTC, which is a civilized afternoon in London, morning in New York, dawn in Los Angeles, evening in Bengaluru, and one o’clock in the morning in Sydney. Drop Sydney and the same window becomes comfortable for everyone left. That is usually the real finding: not which hour to pick, but which person to stop inviting.

How to read the strips

Inside the day

A normal working hour where that person is. Nobody is being asked for a favor.

At the edge

The first or last hour. Fine occasionally, corrosive every week.

Their evening or night

A window that crosses one of these is not a window. The tool will still show you the best of them, labeled.

What it does, and what it does not

  • The zones are real and so is the daylight saving. Local hours come from the browser’s own time zone database through Intl.DateTimeFormat, so Kolkata is half an hour off the grid and London is an hour ahead of Berlin’s winter offset in April, without any of that being written down here.
  • It scores an hour by the worst person in it. A window ranks on how the least well served participant is doing, then on the total. Averaging would let four comfortable people outvote one person at 3am, which is the exact mistake this is meant to stop.
  • Everyone counts the same. There is no way to say that one person’s early start matters less, and that is deliberate. If it does matter less, take them out of the list and see what the window becomes.
  • One day, no calendars. It knows nothing about weekends, holidays, school runs, or what is already booked. It answers “is this hour humane” and not “is this hour free”.
  • Nothing leaves the page. No request is made and nothing is stored. The file works from a USB stick, on a plane, in five years.
The time zone data is the browser’s, which is the IANA database as of whenever it was last updated, so a zone that changed its rules very recently may be wrong here and will be wrong in every other tool on the same machine. The working-hour bands are a convention rather than a measurement: they are what the reader chose from the control above, and the tool has no opinion about anyone’s actual day.
