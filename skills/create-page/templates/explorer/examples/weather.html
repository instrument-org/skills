Three hundred and sixty-five days each, which is enough for the year to have a shape you can see rather than a number you have to trust.

Three things the year says

Sydney's year runs backwards, and Nairobi's barely runs.

London's warmest month is July at a mean high of 28.0°C and its coldest is January at 7.6. Sydney's warmest is February at 27.1 and its coldest is July at 17.0 — the same curve, six months out of phase. Nairobi never leaves a five-degree band all year, which is what being on the equator does: it has a wet season and a dry season rather than a warm one and a cold one.

Reykjavik rains far more often than Tokyo and far less hard.

197 days of rain in Reykjavik against 144 in Tokyo — and 1,406 mm of water against 1,743. One drizzles for more than half the year, the other holds its rain and then drops it: Tokyo's wettest single day, 73.8 mm on 5 September 2025, is more than five percent of its annual total. “Is it rainy” turns out to be two different questions, and the two charts above answer them differently.

The extremes are one day each, and they are the numbers people will quote.

42.0°C in Sydney on 10 January and −18.3°C in New York on 31 January are both single rows out of 2,190. New York's 59-degree spread between its hottest high and its coldest low is real and it is also the least representative fact on this page — its mean high, 17.1°C, is within a third of a degree of London's. A year of data is worth having precisely because it makes that visible.

Method. Daily maximum and minimum two-meter temperature and daily precipitation sum from the Open-Meteo historical archive, one request per city for 1 September 2025 to 31 August 2026 in UTC, read on 10 September 2026. Coordinates are each city’s centre to two decimal places, so these are the conditions in the middle of town rather than at the airport the official figures usually come from, and they will differ by a degree or so from a national met office. The archive is reanalysis: model output corrected against observations, not a thermometer reading, so treat a single day as very good rather than exact. What it cannot answer: anything about whether this year was normal. One year has no baseline in it, and every comparison on this page is between cities rather than between years.
