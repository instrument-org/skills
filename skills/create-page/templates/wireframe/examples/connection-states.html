Wireframe · One panel, six states

The calendar connection, in every state it can actually be in

We designed two states and shipped six. The four nobody drew — expired, half-granted, provider down, and revoked from the other end — are where every support conversation about this feature comes from, and each of them currently renders the same grey box saying “Not connected”. This is the same panel six times, at the same size, so the only thing that changes between frames is the thing being argued about: what the panel says when it is not working, and what it offers to do about it.

, side by side rather than in sequence

Three rules the six frames are an argument for

Name the moment it broke, not the fact that it is broken.

“Reconnect your calendar” asks a person to act with no idea what it cost them. “Nothing has synced since Tuesday, and 6 meetings were missed” is the same request with the reason attached, and it is the difference between an ignored banner and a click. Frames 4 and 6 both carry a date and a count.

Say which feature stopped, in the words of the feature.

A missing scope is not a permissions problem to the person holding it; it is the reason the thing they wanted does not happen. Frame 5 never uses the word scope. It says that we can read the calendar and cannot write to it, so holds do not appear, and offers exactly the one permission that fixes exactly that.

When it is not their fault, do not draw it as their problem.

Frame 6 has no button. An outage at the provider is not something a retry loop fixes, and offering one teaches people to distrust every button in the panel. What it has instead is the last successful sync, so the person knows how stale they are, and a note that we will pick it back up on our own.

Reading these

Every frame is 520×400, the real size of this panel in Settings, drawn at the real type scale. That is deliberate: when the axis is conditions rather than moments, one frame differing in size would read as a difference in the design rather than a difference in the state. Click any frame to open it.

  • Every word in these frames is final-quality. There are no grey bars, because on a panel this size copy is the whole design and a placeholder would hide the only thing worth reviewing.
  • The account name, the dates and the counts are invented. “6 meetings missed” is a shape, not a measurement. What has to be true before we ship this is that we can compute a number like it; if we cannot, the copy has to drop to something we can stand behind.
  • No provider is named or drawn. The panel says “your calendar” throughout, and the marks in the frames are neutral, because the same six states have to hold for every provider we support and drawing one of them invites a design that fits only it.
  • Not drawn: the provider’s own consent screen, which is theirs and not ours to design; the multi-account case; and the admin-disabled state, which is a seventh and probably belongs here.
A proposal, not a record. No screen was measured for these; the panel size is the one Settings uses today and everything inside it is new. The account, the dates and every count are invented to give the copy something the right shape to hold, and the copy is the thing under review. Before any of it ships, the counts in frames 4 and 5 have to be numbers we can actually produce — if they are not, the sentences have to shrink to what we can stand behind rather than the other way round.
